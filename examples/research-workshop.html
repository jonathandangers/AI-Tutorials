Worked example · Project home

Research groups project home

What a long-running project looks like when everything about it lives in one place: a OneDrive folder holds the record, a Copilot workspace holds your instructions, and each task gets its own short thread.

This shows what is possible, not a setup guide. It assumes a premium Microsoft 365 Copilot license, which can work with your OneDrive files directly. Copilot Chat can do the same thread work with files you upload into the conversation.

DraftGroups and details below are fictional. Confirm what your Copilot calls the place that holds standing instructions and saved prompts, and which steps it does directly versus drafting for you to apply.

How the pieces fit

Goal
Keep 8–14 research groups' status, blockers, and next steps current, and walk into every meeting prepared, without re-reading old emails.
Project home
A OneDrive folder: a tracker workbook with one row per group, a folder per group for transcripts and notes, and a folder of approved outputs.
AI workspace
KUMC Copilot, signed in with @kumc.edu. Standing instructions describe the project and point to the folder; skills hold the prompts you repeat.
Threads
One per task: prep a meeting, update after one, think through a stalled group, draft an email. Each ends when its output is approved and saved.
Data rules
Research group materials are internal, so they stay in KUMC tools. See Start here.
Checked
Brief compared against the tracker row and the transcripts; any contradiction resolved before the meeting.

A week in the project

WhenWhat you doWhat gets saved
After a meetingDrop the transcript in the group's folder. In a new thread, run the post-meeting update skill. Check its proposed tracker changes and action items.Updated tracker row; meeting summary in the group folder
Before a meetingRun the prep brief skill for that group.Nothing, unless you want the brief kept
When a group stallsOpen a thread to think it through: what changed, what the options are, what to ask. Push back on easy answers.The decision, in the tracker's notes
MonthlyRun the status report skill across all groups.The approved report
Prep brief skill, in use
Prep brief for Group C, meeting tomorrow. Use their tracker row and the two most recent transcripts in their folder: last meeting date, current stage, open blockers, and what each person committed to last time. Five bullets at most, then two questions I should ask. Flag anything in the transcripts that contradicts the tracker.
Output excerpttrimmed, fictional

Last met: six weeks ago. Stage: data collection.

Committed last time: finish the chart-review form; send the IRB amendment.

Contradiction: the tracker lists the amendment as sent, but the last transcript says it was still being drafted.

Ask: Did the amendment go out? Who is doing the second chart review?

What makes this work
  • The record lives outside the chat. Any thread, or any person, can pick up the project from the folder.
  • Repeated prompts become skills. The prep brief asks the same things every time, so nobody rebuilds it.
  • Threads stay short. One task each, so the model works from the files rather than from a long, noisy conversation.
  • You approve before anything is saved. The tracker only changes after you check the proposed update.

Takeaway

The same pattern fits any project that runs for months: a curriculum, a committee, a grant, a quality project. Keep the record in one folder, keep your instructions and repeated prompts in the AI workspace, and give each task its own short thread.
