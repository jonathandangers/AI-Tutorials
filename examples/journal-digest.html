Worked example · Scheduled tasks

Journal email digest

Journal and society emails pile up faster than anyone reads them. This scheduled task condenses them into one short digest twice a week.

IllustrativeThe instruction below shows the pattern. Edit the sources and topics to match your own inbox.

Goal
Keep up with table-of-contents and alert emails (NEJM, ACCESSSS, specialty society updates) without opening each one.
Tools
An LLM with access to a personal mail account, running on a schedule. Journal alerts are public information.
Does
Merges items that appear in more than one email and drops topics outside my practice.
Delivers
One digest, twice a week, with a link to every article.
Checked
Links open the article, and merged items are the same paper. Anything I plan to use, I read at the source.
Scheduled instruction
Twice a week: read the journal and society alert emails that arrived since the last digest (NEJM, ACCESSSS, and my specialty society). Merge items that appear in more than one email. Keep anything relevant to emergency medicine, medical education, or AI in clinical practice, and skip the rest. For each item give the title, journal, one sentence on the finding, and the link. Put practice-changing items first.

Takeaway

This is a good first automation. The stakes are low, the output comes back to you, and one edit to the instruction changes what you receive. The digest tells you what to read; the article itself is still the source.

Use a personal mail account for this. Have the alerts sent to a personal address. Connecting a personal AI account to KUMC email would expose institutional messages to a tool approved for public information only.
