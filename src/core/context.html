Module 03

Giving the model context

Pocket card

  • State the goal, the audience, the constraints, and what the output should look like.
  • Add the material itself: files, screenshots, transcripts, links, connected sources.
  • When unsure, ask: "What else would you need from me to do this well?"

What to explain

Explain the task the way you would to a capable colleague who just walked into the room. Weak output usually traces back to something the model was never told.

The list below works as a checklist before you send a request. A quick question may need only the goal. A document other people will read usually needs most of the list.

Almost always

  • Your goal. What you are trying to accomplish, and why.
  • Relevant background. What someone new to the task would need to know.
  • Intended audience. Who will read or use the result.

When it matters

  • Important constraints. Deadlines, rules, and things to avoid.
  • Desired format. Email, table, outline, slides.
  • Length and level of detail. One paragraph or five pages.
  • Tone or writing style. Formal, plain, or matched to a sample of your writing.
  • An example or template. Something you want the result to resemble.
  • What you have already tried. And why it fell short.

The same request, with and without context

Illustrative: one meeting transcript, requested two ways.

One line
Summarize this meeting transcript.

The committee discussed several topics, including scheduling, simulation, and the budget. Members shared updates and agreed to follow up on open items.

Accurate, and of little use to anyone who missed the meeting.
With context
For committee members who missed it. Decisions first, then action items with owners and due dates, then open questions. Under one page. Flag agenda items that were skipped. Agenda attached.

Decision: Simulation moves to the second Thursday.

Action: Draft the new sim schedule. Owner needed.

Skipped: Travel budget (agenda item 4).

Someone could act on this.

Ways to add context to your chat

  • Type or paste text
  • Talk or dictate
  • Upload documents or PDFs
  • Add screenshots
  • Add presentations
  • Add meeting transcripts
  • Share URLs
  • Use connected sources

Every item you add follows the data rules in Start here. A transcript of an internal meeting is confidential, so it belongs in KUMC Copilot.

Let the model tell you what is missing

When you are unsure whether you have given enough, end your request with one question. The model will usually name the missing audience, format, or constraint. Answer, then let it start.
