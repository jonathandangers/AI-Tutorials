Module 02

Conversation is only the interface.

An LLM can play several different roles in your work. The chat box is how you direct it, not the limit of what it does.

Pocket card

  • Six roles: thinking partner, tutor, organizer, writer, research assistant, builder.
  • Brain-dump first, organize second. Skip the polished prompt.
  • Ask for the output you actually need: a table, a deck, a file, a database.

Six ways to use an LLM

  • Thinking and strategy partner

    Brainstorm, pressure-test an idea, compare approaches, find blind spots, or simply bounce ideas off someone very capable whenever you need to.

  • Tutor and guide

    Ask it to teach you something you do not know yet, or guide you through a task while you do it. Ask follow-up questions until the fuzzy parts make sense.

  • Organizer for messy thinking

    Brain-dump by typing or voice. Let the LLM turn it into an outline, plan, email, table, or organized set of thoughts. Then react and revise.

  • Writer and editor

    Draft, shorten, restructure, rewrite, or adapt something for a particular audience. You provide the judgment about what is worth saying and whether it sounds right.

  • Research and analysis assistant

    Search connected sources, synthesize information, compare options, extract data, critique reasoning, or help you work through evidence.

  • Builder and operator

    Create files, databases, presentations, sites, simple apps, or workflows, and, with the right tools, interact with the systems where your work lives.

Production or practice? New

Decide at the start of a session which kind of work you are doing, and tell the model.

Production

You want a finished product you would put your name on. AI does much of the drafting. You shape it, push back, iterate, and check it against your standards before it goes anywhere. "Build this, looks good" is not production; see Module 04 for what checking means.

Practice

You want to get better at the task itself. You do the work, and AI coaches: it asks questions, gives hints, and critiques, but does not hand you the answer. Use it when the effort is what builds the skill.

Try: "Quiz me one question at a time; hint before answer." Or: "I'll write it first. Tell me what I missed, but don't rewrite it."

The models default to production and will hand you answers unless you ask for coaching. Some tools have a built-in study mode that does this for you.

Brain-dump first. Organize second.

One of the highest-value habits. You do not need to spend ten minutes turning your thoughts into a polished prompt. Type or dictate the messy version. Ask the LLM to organize it. Then react: "keep this," "move that," "this is too formal," "I forgot something." The LLM does the friction-heavy work of writing and reorganizing while you supply the ideas and judgment.
