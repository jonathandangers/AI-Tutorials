Going deeper

Models, reasoning, and usage

Pocket card

  • Use the most capable model, with more reasoning, for large projects, format conversions, and planning.
  • A fast model is enough for short rewrites and simple questions.
  • Check your usage when it matters, and keep experimenting either way.

Model choice

Most platforms offer a faster, lighter model and a slower, more capable one. The faster model handles short rewrites and simple questions well. The more capable model earns its cost on work with many parts that have to stay consistent.

Three kinds of work where I reach for the most capable model:

  • Large projects across several documents. A lecture series or a teaching site where a change in one file has to carry through to the others.
  • Converting material into a new format. Turning a Word document of lecture notes into a visual HTML page, or a chapter into slides with speaker notes.
  • Strategic planning. Laying out a curriculum, sequencing a project, or weighing options before committing time to one.

Reasoning level

Many platforms also let you choose how long the model thinks before answering. Give the hard problems above more thinking time. For a simple task, extra reasoning mostly adds waiting.

Usage

Capable models, high reasoning, tool use, and large amounts of context all draw more from your plan's limits. If you are running short, check the usage page and move routine work to the faster model. While you are still learning what a workflow can do, favor trying it over rationing.
