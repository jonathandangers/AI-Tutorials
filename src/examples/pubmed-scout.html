PubMed scout

A recurring workflow that prepares work for you to review.

Goal
Learn about new publications on the topics I teach without searching every week.
Tools
Claude connected to PubMed, running as a weekly scheduled task.
Delivers
An email with useful papers, why each may matter to a specific topic, and links.
Inputs
A public topic list: the subjects I follow, in plain words. No unpublished work or study data.
Your job
Decide what is relevant and verify anything you plan to use.
Checked
Every link opens the PubMed record, and each summary matches the abstract before a paper is used.
Scheduled instruction
Every Monday: search PubMed for papers from the past 7 days on each topic in my public topic list. For each useful paper, give the citation, a two-sentence summary, which topic it bears on and why, and the PubMed link. Skip anything that is only loosely related. Email the result to me.
Output excerptone paper

Intravenous thrombolysis for acute central retinal artery occlusion: protocol for a systematic review and individual participant data meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Mac Grory B, et al. PLoS One, 2026. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0342739 · PMID 41805966

Why it matters: Bears on the eye emergencies topic. The pooled analysis will address whether IV thrombolysis within 4.5 hours improves vision in CRAO, a question the current lecture describes as unsettled. This is a protocol, so there are no results yet.

Takeaway

The automation prepares material for your review and takes no action on its own. It searches PubMed, so the citations start from real records, which makes invented papers much less likely. A summary can still misstate a paper. Open the link and check the abstract before you use anything.
